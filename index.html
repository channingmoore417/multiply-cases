Why We Only Work With Smaller Markets

Because that's where we can actually compete.

If you're in a major metro with millions of people, you're competing with firms that can spend more on marketing every month than they pay an associate attorney in an entire year. We're not going to pretend a $197/month website is going to overpower that kind of budget.

But a market under 500,000 people is a different game.

You're usually competing against a much smaller group of local firms. And a lot of those firms have outdated websites, neglected Google profiles, and no real strategy behind how they show up when someone searches for a lawyer.

That's the opportunity.

You don't need to be the biggest firm in the state. You need to be one of the firms people find when they search for a lawyer in your city, parish, or county.

What we consider a small market

Generally, we're looking for firms where:

  • Your city or metro has 500,000 people or fewer.
  • You're competing with a manageable number of local firms, not hundreds.
  • The firms outranking you are other local practices, not national firms with enormous marketing budgets.
  • Your goal is to own your local market, rather than spend a fortune trying to compete across an entire state.
